Privacy

Mobile Operator, the iPhone app and the Mac app. Last updated 2026-09-22 (evening).

What the app records

When you hold the dot and speak, the phone records audio and sends it securely to our own transcription server, which turns it into text and does not keep the audio: it is deleted as soon as the text is made. The words that come back are yours: they are typed where your cursor is, on your Mac or in the app you are using on your iPhone, or kept in the app's History for later.

What we store, and the choice you have

One switch, in Settings under "Share your dictations": on by default, and off means nothing below is written anywhere, not even a counter.

While it is on, each dictation's text and a copy of its audio are stored under an anonymous account created the first time you open the app (no name, no email, no phone number attached), so we can improve the speech model. Turning the switch off stops this for everything you say afterwards; it does not reach back and delete what was already sent — write to the address below if you want a past dictation deleted.

What we do not do

We do not sell your data. We do not use it for advertising, and the app has no advertising or tracking library in it: nothing here is linked to you across other companies' apps or websites.

The Mac and the iPhone, to each other

Pairing a phone with a Mac exchanges a device name, a pairing code and the anonymous account id between those two devices only, over your own network. That exchange never reaches us.

Diagnostics

If the app crashes, it sends us the crash report (which part of the code failed, the build, the device model and system version), and once a day a summary of how much battery, processor and network the app used. Neither contains anything you said or typed. Reporting a bug opens a plain email to us with your build number, iOS version and device model in it, sent only when you choose to send it.
